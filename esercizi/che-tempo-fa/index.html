Che tempo fa?

Il tempo in italiano: le parole, la regola di fa e c'è, e un meteo da presentare in due. The weather in Italian: the words, the fa / c'è rule, and a weather forecast to present to each other. Best with a partner, works alone too.

Le parole del tempo

Ten phrases, that's all you need. Say each one out loud, with the gesture if you like (brr for fa freddo). Tap 🔊 to hear it.

Che tempo fa?

Look at the picture and pick the phrase. Then say it out loud.

Fa, c'è o da solo?

In Italian the weather takes fa, c'è or nothing at all. Say the whole sentence out loud («Fa caldo!»), then tap the box. 60 seconds: how many can you sort?

1:00
caldo

hot

fa ...

fa caldo, fa freddo, fa bel tempo, fa brutto tempo

c'è ...

c'è il sole, c'è vento, c'è la nebbia

da solo

piove, nevica (no it, no fa: just the verb)

The English trap: it is hot is not «è caldo». It's fa caldo. And the question is always Che tempo fa? Only one with è: è nuvoloso, it's cloudy.

Il meteo

You are on Italian TV. One of you is the presenter and reads the weather, the other listens and puts it on the cards. Then swap. Alone? Choose «Play alone»: you read the forecast and rebuild it.

1:00

1 · Presenter only

Don't show the screen! Look at the forecast, then tap Hide and present and tell your partner: «Buongiorno! Oggi a Milano piove...»

2 · Listener

Now give the phone to your partner. Listener: tap the weather for each city. Ask «Come?» or «Che tempo fa a Roma?» if you need to.

Da voi

Now the real weather. Look out of the window and build your sentence, then say it to each other. Every day this week, ask each other: Che tempo fa oggi?


La missione

At the start of the next lesson, each of you tells Fabio in 30 seconds: the weather where you are today and the weather in a city you'd love to be in. «Oggi qui piove e fa freddo. A Palermo invece c'è il sole e fa caldo!»
